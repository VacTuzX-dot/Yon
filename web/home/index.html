Yon (โยน, “to toss”) sends files between your Macs, PCs and phones. Straight from one device to the other, encrypted, with no account and no cloud.

  3. Checked, and whole again

What it does

  • Finds devices on your Wi-Fi. Pick one, pick files or a whole folder, and the other side chooses Accept or Decline.
  • Any file, any size. Large files stream, so a 1 GB video uses a few MB of memory, and every file is checked with SHA-256 when it arrives.
  • Phones too, no app store. Scan a QR code once, add the page to your Home Screen, and send photos from your phone to your computer.
  • From anywhere, if you want. Turn on Reach from anywhere and your phone reaches your computer on any network, through a relay that only ever sees encrypted data.

The builds aren't notarized by Apple or signed for Windows yet, so the first open from a download asks you to confirm: macOS, Privacy & Security → Open Anyway; Windows, More info → Run anyway.

Pair a phone

  1. On the computer, open Settings → Phones → Pair a phone.
  2. Scan the QR code with the phone's camera and open the link.
  3. Add the page to your Home Screen. From then on, tap the Yon icon to send.

How it stays private

  • Every install has its own Ed25519 key, and computers talk over mutual TLS 1.3. The receiver sees the sender's device code, not just a name that could be faked.
  • Nothing is written until you accept, and file names are cleaned so they can't escape the save folder or overwrite anything.
  • Phones encrypt every request with their pairing key (ChaCha20-Poly1305). The relay forwards sealed data and stores nothing.
  • Updates are signed and checked against a key built into the app.

Details, trade-offs and what's still missing are in the README and the threat models.
